Two traders can use the same strategy on the same market and get opposite results. The difference is usually not the system; it is what each of them does after three losses in a row. Trading psychology is less about mindset slogans and more about building routines that make good decisions automatic.

## Know the usual traps

- **Revenge trading.** Increasing size or trading more often to win back a loss quickly.
- **Fear of missing out.** Jumping into a move after it has already run, without a plan for where you are wrong.
- **Moving the stop.** Pushing a stop-loss further away because you "know" the market will turn.
- **Cutting winners early.** Taking a small profit to feel safe, while letting losses run to their full size.
- **Overconfidence after a streak.** Treating a few good trades as proof that the rules no longer apply.

Each one turns a defined risk into an undefined one.

## Write rules before the session, not during it

Decisions made in the moment are made under pressure. Decide in advance:

1. Which markets and setups you will trade.
2. How much you risk per trade and per day.
3. When you stop trading, for example after two losses or after reaching your daily limit.
4. What has to be true for you to enter, written as a short checklist.

Keep the rules visible on your desk or next to the platform.

## Keep a trading journal

For every trade, record the setup, the reason for entering, the planned stop and target, the result and one line on how you felt. After twenty or thirty trades, patterns appear: maybe your Friday afternoon trades lose, or you break rules after a big win. A journal turns vague feelings into evidence you can act on.

## Separate the decision from the outcome

A well-planned trade that hits its stop was still a good trade. A rule-breaking trade that happened to win was still a bad one. Judge yourself on whether you followed the process. Over a large number of trades, the process is what produces the results.

## Size so that losses do not hurt

If a single loss makes you anxious, the position is too big. Use the [lot size calculator](/tools/lot-size-calculator) and a risk rule small enough that one trade does not decide your week. Smaller size reduces stress, and lower stress makes it much easier to follow the plan.

## Build a routine

- **Before:** check the [economic calendar](/tools/economic-calendar), mark key levels and list the trades you would take.
- **During:** only take trades from your list; step away after hitting a stop rule.
- **After:** update the journal and note one thing to improve.

## When to take a break

Stop for the day after your loss limit, when you notice you are trading to "get it back", or when you feel tired or distracted. The market will be there tomorrow; your capital needs to be too.

Discipline is not a personality trait you either have or lack. It is the result of rules, size and routines that make the disciplined choice the easy one.

Most traders who blow up an account do not fail because their ideas were bad. They fail because one or two losing trades were simply too big. Risk management is the part of trading you fully control, and it starts before you press buy.

## Decide your risk per trade first

Pick a fixed share of your account that you are willing to lose on any single idea. Many traders use somewhere between 0.5% and 2%. The exact number is personal; what matters is that it is small and consistent.

Why small? Because losing streaks are normal. Risking 1% per trade, ten losses in a row costs about 10% of the account. Risking 10% per trade, the same streak leaves you with roughly a third of what you started with, and you would need to triple it just to get back.

## Put the stop where the idea is wrong

A stop-loss is not a number you pick to feel comfortable. It belongs at the price where your reason for the trade no longer holds: beyond a support level for a long position, above resistance for a short, or outside the recent range for a breakout.

Only after the stop is in the right place do you work out the position size.

## The position sizing formula

> Lots = (Account balance × Risk %) ÷ (Stop distance in pips × Value of one pip per lot)

**Example.** You have $8,000 and risk 1%, so $80. Your stop on GBP/USD is 32 pips away and one pip on one lot is worth $10.

- $80 ÷ (32 × $10) = 0.25 lots.

If the stop is hit, you lose about $80, exactly as planned. A wider stop automatically means a smaller position, and a tighter stop a larger one. The [lot size calculator](/tools/lot-size-calculator) applies this formula to every instrument and account currency.

## Think in reward-to-risk

Before entering, compare the distance to your target with the distance to your stop. A target twice as far as your stop is a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio. With 2:1 trades you can be right less than half the time and still come out ahead over many trades. With 1:2 trades you need to be right far more often.

## Watch the risks you cannot see on the chart

- **Correlation.** Buying EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD at once is closer to one big bet against the dollar than three separate trades.
- **Events.** Central bank decisions and major data releases can move prices through your stop. Check the [economic calendar](/tools/economic-calendar) before you trade.
- **Weekends.** Prices can open on Monday far from Friday's close. A stop does not guarantee the exact exit price.
- **Leverage.** High leverage lets you open more than your plan allows. Your risk rule, not the margin available, should decide size.

## Set daily and weekly limits

Add a limit such as "I stop trading for the day after losing 3%". It protects you from revenge trading, the urge to win back losses quickly, which is behind many of the worst days traders have.

## A simple checklist

1. Where is my stop, and why there?
2. What is my risk in money, and is it within my rule?
3. What size does that give me?
4. Is there a major event before my target is likely to be reached?
5. How does this trade overlap with positions I already hold?

If you cannot answer all five, the trade is not ready yet. For the mindset side of sticking to these rules, read [trading psychology](/blog/trading-psychology).

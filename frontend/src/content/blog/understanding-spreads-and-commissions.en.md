Every trade has a cost before the market moves at all. Brokers charge it in one of two ways: inside the **spread**, or as a raw spread plus a separate **commission**. Knowing how to compare the two helps you pick the right account and judge whether a strategy can be profitable.

## The spread is a cost you pay on entry

The spread is the gap between the bid and ask. You buy at the ask and can only sell at the bid, so a fresh position is behind by exactly the spread. On one standard lot of EUR/USD, each pip of spread costs about $10.

Spreads are **variable**. They are usually tightest when markets are busiest and can widen around major news, at the daily rollover and when liquidity is thin, such as late on Friday or during holidays.

## Commission accounts: raw spread plus a fee

On a raw-pricing account, the spread is passed through close to the underlying market and a fixed commission is charged per lot instead. That tends to suit traders who trade often or in size, because a fixed fee per lot can work out cheaper than a wider spread.

## Comparing the two

Convert everything to the cost of a full trade (open and close) on the same volume.

Using Bolt Markets' minimum spreads on EUR/USD for one standard lot:

| Account | Minimum spread | Spread cost | Commission | Minimum total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | 1.5 pips | $15 | none | $15 |
| Pro | 1.0 pip | $10 | none | $10 |
| Institutional | 0.0 pips | $0 | $3 per lot | from $3 per lot |

These are **minimums**. The real spread at the moment you trade will often be higher, and how commission adds up depends on whether it is charged once or on each side of the trade. Use the cost estimator on the [trading accounts page](/trading/accounts) to compare accounts for your own volume.

## How costs shape a strategy

A scalper aiming for 5 pips per trade gives away a large share of each win to a 1.5-pip spread. A swing trader targeting 150 pips barely notices it. Before choosing an account, ask:

- **How many trades** do I place in a month?
- **What is my average target** in pips?
- **How large** are my positions?

The shorter your targets and the higher your volume, the more a raw-spread account with commission tends to make sense.

## Other costs to know

- **Swaps.** Positions held past the daily rollover receive or pay an overnight financing adjustment. Check the current rates in MetaTrader 5 under Symbol → Specification.
- **Slippage.** In fast markets an order can fill at a different price from the one you saw. It can work for or against you.
- **Payment fees.** Any fee charged by a payment method is shown before you confirm a deposit.

## Bottom line

Compare accounts on the total cost of the trades you actually make, not on the headline spread alone. Then make sure your targets are large enough that costs are a small part of each result.

Gold is one of the most traded instruments on any multi-asset platform. It pays no interest and has no earnings, so its price is driven almost entirely by what other assets offer and by how nervous investors feel. Four forces explain most of its moves.

## 1. Real interest rates

Holding gold means giving up the interest you could earn elsewhere. What matters is the **real** yield: the interest rate minus expected inflation. When real yields rise, the opportunity cost of holding gold goes up and the price often comes under pressure. When real yields fall, gold tends to look more attractive.

That is why inflation releases and central bank decisions frequently move gold as much as they move currencies.

## 2. The US dollar

Gold is priced in dollars (XAU/USD). A stronger dollar makes gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies, which can weigh on demand; a weaker dollar can have the opposite effect. The relationship is not fixed, but it is one of the first things gold traders check.

## 3. Central bank and investment demand

Central banks hold gold as part of their reserves, and changes in their buying can shape long-term trends. Investment funds that hold physical gold also add or remove demand as investors move money in and out. These flows tend to matter over weeks and months rather than minutes.

## 4. Risk sentiment

In periods of financial stress or geopolitical tension, investors often look for assets they see as stores of value. Gold can rally sharply on these days, sometimes even when yields and the dollar point the other way.

## How gold behaves as an instrument

- **Contract size.** On MetaTrader 5, one lot of XAU/USD typically represents 100 troy ounces. Check the exact size in the [commodities contract specifications](/markets/commodities).
- **Point value.** On one lot, a $1 move in the gold price is worth about $100. Use the [pip calculator](/tools/pip-calculator) for your own size.
- **Volatility.** Gold can move tens of dollars in minutes around US data. Size positions accordingly.
- **Sessions.** Gold trades almost around the clock on weekdays, with a short daily break and the most activity during the London and New York sessions.

## A practical checklist

1. Check the dollar and recent moves in yields.
2. Look at the [economic calendar](/tools/economic-calendar) for inflation, employment and central bank events.
3. Mark the levels where your idea would be wrong and size from that stop.
4. Avoid holding oversized positions into major releases.

Gold rewards traders who respect its volatility. Start small, plan every exit and let the four drivers above tell you why the price is moving, not just where.
